How the calculators work

The method and assumptions behind each result, so you can check them.

SIP

Paid at the start of each month. The monthly rate is the yearly return divided by 12. Value = amount × ((1 + i)ⁿ − 1) ÷ i × (1 + i), where n is the number of months.

Lump sum

Compounded once a year. Value = amount × (1 + r)ʸ.

Step-up SIP

As SIP, month by month, with the monthly amount raised by your chosen percentage every 12 months.

Future value

A starting amount compounded yearly, plus a contribution at the end of each year.

SWP

The yearly return is converted to an effective monthly rate, (1 + r)^(1/12) − 1. Growth is applied first, then the withdrawal at month end. Withdrawals stop when the money runs out.

PMS

The expected return compounds once a year. Each year the fixed fee is taken as a share of the year-end value, then the performance fee takes its share of any gain above the hurdle, measured from the highest value reached so far. Before tax.

AIF

A simplified model: the whole commitment is treated as invested from the start and compounds yearly. The management fee is charged on the commitment every year. At exit, carry takes its share of the profit above a hurdle that compounds yearly, with no catch-up. Real funds draw and return capital in stages.

Invoice discounting

Simple interest for each cycle on a 365-day year: amount × rate × days ÷ 365. If reinvested, each payout is added to the next cycle. Assumes every invoice is paid on time.

P2P monthly returns

A level monthly instalment worked out from the IRR as an effective monthly rate. Each receipt includes your own principal. Assumes no defaults or delays.

Term cover

Annual income × years until retirement, plus loans, less existing cover and savings. A rule of thumb, not an underwriting decision.

Goal, education and wedding

Today’s cost is raised by inflation to the target year. Savings you already hold grow at the expected return, compounded yearly. The SIP for the remaining gap uses the SIP method above.

Retirement

Monthly expenses are raised by inflation to the retirement date. The amount needed at retirement funds those expenses, still rising with inflation, until the age you plan to, at the return after retirement. Existing savings grow yearly at the return before retirement, and the SIP covers the shortfall.

Results are before tax, costs and charges unless stated. Every result is an illustration, not a forecast or guarantee. No result is a recommendation of any product or an assessment of what suits you.
